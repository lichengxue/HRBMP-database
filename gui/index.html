Program Description

HRBMP Program Overview

Purpose

This GUI organizes HRBMP information around monitoring stations, sampling events, biological observations, environmental observations, and source metadata. The portal keeps the workflow transparent for scientific users while the database structure is still being refined.

Data Workflow

  1. Load validated source tables into SQLite.
  2. Export analysis-ready JSON summaries for the static GUI.
  3. Review coverage by station, time period, taxonomic group, and data type.
  4. Update records with reviewed HRBMP data and documentation.
01

Stations

Location, river mile, region, and station notes.

02

Sampling Events

Sampling date, gear, program, depth, and event metadata.

03

Biological Observations

Taxa, counts, abundance units, and specimen preservation fields.

04

Environmental Observations

HRBMP, USGS, EPA, and NOAA database variables.

05

Metadata Sources

File provenance, contacts, institutions, and date-added fields.

The HRBMP Surveys

HRBMP uses distinct survey programs with different gear types to represent fish across life stages and river regions, with major survey coverage extending along the Hudson River between Albany and New York City.

Educational Materials

K-12 Curriculum

A Hudson River learning pathway organized by student age, classroom readiness, and scientific skill level.

Curriculum Pathway

The sequence moves from observation and vocabulary in early grades to authentic data analysis and evidence-based environmental decision-making in high school.

K-2 Grades 3-5 Grades 6-8 Grades 9-12 Teacher Resources
K-2: River Explorers

Observation-rich lessons for young students learning what living things need and how animals use body parts to survive.

Meet Hudson River Animals

Students sort fish, crabs, and other river organisms by observable features, then draw what each animal needs to live.

Fish Body Parts

Students label fins, eyes, mouth, tail, and body shape, then explain how those parts help a fish move, feed, and hide.

Grades 3-5: Habitat Detectives

Elementary investigations that connect life cycles, habitat, river regions, and simple graphs from monitoring records.

Fish Life Stages

Students compare eggs, larvae, juveniles, and adults, then match life stages to habitat needs in the estuary.

Where Are The Fish?

Students use HRBMP river regions to map species observations and build simple bar charts by region or season.

Grades 6-8: Estuary Data Lab

Middle school modules where students ask questions, filter database records, interpret patterns, and connect fish distributions to environmental conditions.

Biodiversity By Region

Students compare species richness and abundance across Hudson River regions and discuss how sampling design affects conclusions.

Water Quality And Fish

Students compare temperature, dissolved oxygen, salinity, and fish records to identify possible habitat relationships.

Grades 9-12: Hudson River Research Lab

High school investigations focused on authentic monitoring questions, data limitations, evidence, and communication of scientific claims.

Long-Term Change

Students analyze annual or seasonal patterns in selected species and evaluate whether the evidence supports a trend claim.

From Data To Decision

Students build a short research brief using database results, uncertainty, and data-sharing expectations for responsible science.

Teacher Resources

Planning materials that can be added as downloadable packets as the curriculum matures.

Lesson Packet Templates

Space for printable lessons, student worksheets, answer keys, vocabulary lists, and slide decks.

Standards Alignment

Space for NY P-12 and NGSS-aligned summaries covering practices, ecosystems, Earth systems, and human activity.

History of HRBMP

How The Program Began

HRBMP was created to improve understanding of Hudson River estuary fisheries during a period of power-plant development and cooling-water intake concerns. The program connects river history, fishery resources, survey design, and long-term monitoring records.

Access Model

Public users can browse approved summaries and availability layers. Registered and approved users receive dataset-specific permissions. Internal management remains restricted to data managers and admins.

  • Deny access unless a dataset policy explicitly allows it.
  • Keep public exports separate from restricted working data.
  • Log restricted downloads and data-management actions.
